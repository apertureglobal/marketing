The Approach

The house is finished. The buyer is close by.

Your home is not advertised everywhere. It is advertised where it matters. A house of this kind — five bedrooms and about 3,800 square feet on more than an acre, at the end of a West Meade cul-de-sac, taken back to the studs in 2022 and rebuilt — is bought by a family already living in and around Nashville who need more room, more quiet, and a house they do not have to renovate. That family is the first audience. The second is smaller: households moving to Nashville from elsewhere who want an established neighbourhood rather than a new one. The campaign is built in two parts so each is approached on its own terms.

Where It Runs

The right buyers already live close to home.

The greater part of the campaign stays in the Nashville metropolitan area, across Davidson, Williamson, Sumner, Wilson and Rutherford counties. That is deliberate. A family moving up to five bedrooms and an acre in West Meade is almost always moving from a smaller house nearby: they already know the schools, the drive downtown, and what an established street is worth. The remaining effort goes to four cities — Chicago, Dallas, Atlanta and Los Angeles — that send households to Nashville in steady numbers, and where a finished house on a private acre reads as good value rather than an extravagance. A wider national footprint would be the wrong shape for this home, and it is not used.
