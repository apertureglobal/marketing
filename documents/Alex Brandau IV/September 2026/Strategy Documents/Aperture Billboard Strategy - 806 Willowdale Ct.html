The Market

One television market, forty-nine counties wide.

806 Willowdale Court stands in Russellwood Heights, in Oak Hill, seven miles south of downtown Nashville and inside Davidson County. Advertising of this kind is bought by market rather than by address, and the market this property sits in is Nashville — forty-nine counties across two states, reaching from the Kentucky line above Hopkinsville down to the Alabama border, and from the Tennessee River in the west to the Cumberland Plateau in the east.

Davidson County is drawn in blue on the map opposite. The other forty-eight counties of the market are shaded, and the whole footprint is outlined as one shape. The pale counties beyond that line sit outside this market; they are reached by the online advertising instead.

Why The Whole Market
The buyer for this house is almost certainly already driving these roads. They are in Green Hills or Forest Hills and have run out of garden; they are in Brentwood or Franklin — which are Williamson County, not Davidson — and want the shorter run into town without giving up the land; or they are moving to Nashville and believe two level acres with a pool no longer exist inside the county. The whole market is targeted because that is the ground this audience moves across.

The Argument

Land and quiet, without leaving the city.

Most houses offering two level acres, a pool and no homeowners association ask a buyer to drive out of Davidson County to reach them. This one does not. Radnor Lake is under two miles away in a straight line, the interstate a little over two, Green Hills under four, and downtown Nashville seven. The Williamson County line is one and seven tenths of a mile from the front door — close enough that the house sits among the schools, shops and commutes people move south for, while the address, the county and the taxes remain Nashville’s. That is the whole of the argument, and out-of-home carries it unusually well: the people it is for are, by definition, already in a car somewhere in this market.

01
The land is the headline, not the house
A four-bedroom brick Colonial is not scarce here. Just under two level acres of it, with a gunite pool, brick terraces and a pool house, inside the Davidson County line and with no association to answer to, is. Every frame leads with the grounds.
02
The audience does not know this exists
Nobody searches for a thing they believe is gone. That is what out-of-home is good for and a property portal is not: it reaches people who are not looking, as they drive past the sort of lot they had given up on.
03
The geography is Davidson and its ring
This is a Nashville house for a Nashville buyer. The emphasis is Davidson County and the counties immediately around it, not a national footprint.
04
One idea per screen, one address for all of them
A driver has a few seconds. Each frame carries the grounds, the acreage, the price and the web address, and nothing else. Every screen ends at the same short address, so a glance from a car can be recovered from a phone that evening.

The Targeting

Chosen by area, not by billboard.

These screens do not work the way billboards used to. We do not take one sign on one road for a month. They are bought programmatically: we tell the exchange which parts of the market to serve the property into, and it places the advertisement on whatever screens are free there while the campaign runs. There is no list of board addresses in this document, and there will not be one. What we choose is the geography.

In practice that means roadside and public digital screens across the forty-nine counties on the previous page, weighted towards the five areas below.

Where The Emphasis Sits
Areas 01 and 02 take the largest share of delivery: one is the ground the house stands on, the other the county whose households most want what it offers. The rest of Davidson takes the next share, the commuter ring a smaller one, and the remaining counties a lower, steady share so that nowhere is dark.
We would rather run everywhere in the market for the full thirty days at a measured weight than crowd two weeks and then disappear. One consequence of buying this way is worth saying plainly: we cannot promise a photograph of your house on a named sign at a named junction. What we can say is which parts of Tennessee and Kentucky saw it, and how often.
