The Geography

Fifteen acres, half an hour from Orlando.

That combination is the whole argument, and it is rare enough to define the footprint. Your property sits in unincorporated Seminole County, on Lake Mills, with a barn, four paddocks and a sand arena — and Orlando International eighteen miles away. Land like this exists in Ocala and in Wellington. It does not exist inside a thirty‑minute drive of downtown Orlando. So the mail is not sent outward to find wealth; it is sent inward, to the households already living in this metropolitan area who need acreage and cannot presently get it.

Who This Is For

The barn changes the list.

A conventional pull for a three and a half million dollar house sorts the region by assessed value and mails the top of it. That list would be wrong here, and expensively so. What is for sale is not principally a five thousand seven hundred square foot residence; it is four stalls, four paddocks, a sand arena in post‑and‑rail fencing, a dock with a boat lift, and fifteen acres behind a seven hundred foot gated drive. The person who wants that is identified by what they keep, not by what their current house is assessed at — and the two lists overlap far less than you would expect.

The Market We Deliberately Leave Out
Ocala is seventy‑three miles north and it is the obvious omission. It is left out on purpose. An Ocala owner already has the land, the barn and the arena; what they do not have is a reason to move thirty minutes from downtown Orlando. The same logic removes Naples and Boca Raton, which are far away and priced above this property. Wellington is the one distant market we keep, and page 05 says why.

The Footprint

Four audiences, one drop.

The four tiers are not four sizes of the same list. Two are asked to produce a buyer. One is asked to produce a conversation — the neighbour along Lake Mills Road who mentions it at the barn. One is a considered bet on the single market in Florida where land is scarcer than money. They are screened differently because they are doing different work, and they are reported separately so the difference is visible afterwards.

Why Five Hundred, And Where It Was Cut
Five hundred pieces in a single drop is the Aperture standard. Five hundred is a small number at this price, and it only works if the geography is cut rather than thinned. So Ocala, Naples and Boca Raton come out altogether, and the whole of the coast comes out; the pieces go instead into a metropolitan area you can reach in half an hour, where an equestrian signal can be screened for directly. Every tier here is capped, and the cap is reached by ranking — never by widening the geography until the count fills.

Qualification

Banded on price, not floored.

Two value bands, because the two purchase tiers are looking for two different things. The luxury belt is bracketed tightly around your asking price. The equestrian tier keeps that same ceiling and drops the floor to roughly four tenths of asking, because a household buying land is buying acreage rather than trading square footage, and their present house understates them.

The tight band runs from roughly six tenths to one and six tenths of the asking price. An open threshold of a million and above would look generous and behave badly: it pulls in every estate far above this one, households that will never trade down into fifteen acres in Seminole County. Bracketing keeps the list to people for whom this is a lateral move or a modest step up — the single change that most improves response on a mailing this small.

Applied Everywhere

Suppression — Applied Before Any Cap
Suppress 746 Lake Mills Road and the adjoining parcel, so this household does not receive its own mailing. Suppress the listing agent’s own address. Suppress the Aperture Global office address. Then deduplicate the four tiers against one another, and only then apply the caps.
Appends On Every Record
Occupation detail, property type, income rank, purchase amount range, length of residence, and the equestrian and boating indicators themselves — so response can be segmented after the fact rather than guessed at. Add a tier code at export; it is what makes per‑market reporting possible at all.
The Ranking Is The Decision
These pulls over‑return heavily — a single affluent Orlando zip will commonly hand back two thousand records against a target of a hundred and sixty. The thresholds define the pool; the sort selects the list. It is specified above and should not be left to the operator.

What Happens Next

Mail fewer, mail better.

The strategy is settled: five hundred pieces, four tiers, one drop, weighted to the households that ride. What follows is the order of work, and the single item that is still a matter of scheduling rather than judgement.

The Footprint
Set. Four tiers, weighted 90 / 180 / 160 / 70, with more than half the delivery on the two equestrian tiers — because the barn, the arena and the paddocks are what distinguish this property from every other lakefront house in the region. Ocala, Naples, Boca Raton and the coast are excluded.
The List
Pulled to the specification on page 07. Suppressed against 746 Lake Mills Road and the adjoining parcel, and against the agent and office addresses; deduplicated across all four tiers; then capped by ranking, not by widening. Tier codes travel with the export so response is read market by market.
The Piece
A 6 × 10.5 in oversized card. The aerial is the image that carries this property — fifteen acres, the arena, the paddocks and the shoreline in one frame — and it needs a format that holds all of it at legible scale.
The Drop Date
The one item still open, and open only because it is scheduling. Fixed against print and list lead times inside the 21 September – 21 October window, and confirmed to you once the pull returns.
